# Amalgamic — Website Copy
Draft v3 · Content only, no design direction · Reference: monarch.com structure

**What changed in v3.** Two structural problems in v2, both flagged by running it against conversion copywriting principles. First, every section was organised around what the product does rather than around a situation the reader recognises, so a visitor had to translate features into their own life before deciding. Second, there was nowhere for a visitor to point at the page and say "that's me". v3 adds a use-case section, a differentiation section that names what genuinely nothing else does, and a before/after line on every feature so the benefit is stated rather than implied. Headlines moved from descriptive to outcome-led. The CTA changed.

---

## Working assumptions

Stated here so they're easy to correct. Everything downstream depends on them.

**Primary action:** connect at least one credit card. Every page drives to this. Nothing else competes with it.

**Ideal customer:** US-based, holds two or more annual-fee cards (Amex Platinum, Chase Sapphire Reserve, Venture X), household income high enough that a $45 fee isn't worth an afternoon, delegates by default.

**The problem in their words:** not "I need to budget better". Closer to "I know I'm leaving money on the table and I can't be bothered to go get it", or "I have no idea what my Platinum actually gives me", or "I found a subscription I cancelled two years ago still charging me".

**Their objections, in the order they arrive:** Can you take my money? Do you see my password? Is this worth the subscription fee? Will it actually finish the job or just tell me about it? What happens to my data?

**Key transformation:** from noticing small financial losses and doing nothing, to not having to notice them at all.

**Proof available today:** mechanism and real recoveries. Not testimonial volume, not brand-name logos.

---

## Voice guide

Write like a competent person explaining something they've thought about, to someone smart who is busy.

Specific over impressive. "A $45 late fee that should have been waived" beats "unlocking hidden value". Numbers where you have them, silence where you don't.

Understated on capability, precise on limits. Say what the product cannot do, early and plainly. In fintech, admitting the boundary is what makes the rest believable.

Second person, present tense, short sentences. No "revolutionise", "seamless", "empower", "unlock", "game-changing", "effortless", "streamline", "optimise". No exclamation marks. No countdown urgency.

Headlines can be bolder. Body copy stays plain. CTAs stay action-oriented.

## 3. How it works

**H2:**
### Connect once. Decide when it matters. That's your whole involvement.

**01 · Connect a card** (about two minutes)

**02 · We tell you what changed** (nothing required from you)
Amalgamic reads statements, transactions and bureau data continuously and surfaces the two or three things that are different this month. Not a dashboard to interpret. A short list.

**03 · Ask about any of it** (as much or as little as you want)

**04 · Hand it over** (one tap)

## 4. Insights

**Eyebrow:** Things to know

**H2:**
### Know what changed this month without opening a spreadsheet.

**Body:**
Most finance apps hand you a dashboard and leave the interpretation to you, which is the same as handing you homework. Amalgamic reads everything and tells you only what's different, in language that doesn't need decoding.

**What it catches, and why you care:**

**Fees you shouldn't have paid.** Late fees, foreign transaction fees, over-limit charges, the annual fee that posted while you were deciding whether to keep the card.
*What that means:* you find out while the fee is still disputable, not when you're reconciling in April.

**Charges that moved.** A merchant billing more than last month. A subscription that renewed at a new price. A charge that appeared twice.

**Credit health.** Utilisation drifting toward the level that moves your score, payment streaks, changes in your bureau file.
*What that means:* you find out before the score moves, not after a lender tells you.

**The line that ties it together:**

> **After:** you're told about it on the 4th, and it's disputed by the 6th.

## 5. The assistant

**Eyebrow:** Ask anything about your money

**H2:**
### An assistant that has actually read your statements.

**Body:**
Most AI assistants can explain what an APR is. This one tells you what yours is costing you, because it's working from your accounts rather than from the internet. Ask in plain language, get an answer with your numbers attached.

**What people ask it:**

> What did I spend on travel last quarter?
> Why is this month's statement higher than last month's?
> If I only pay the minimum on this card, how long until it's clear and what does it cost me?
> Have I been charged by this merchant before?
> Is this fee something I can get waived?

**What's behind it:**

**It reads statements you upload.** Attach a PDF and it parses and analyses the contents, including statements from accounts you haven't connected.

**It audits on request.** Ask it to look for hidden fees, duplicate charges or unusual spikes and it runs a deep scan across every connected account, rather than answering from whatever is already on screen.

**It does the arithmetic you'd avoid.** Minimum payment projections, total interest, what an extra $200 a month actually changes.

**It looks things up and shows its source.** When the answer depends on a merchant's refund window or an issuer's current policy, it searches and cites where the answer came from, so you can check it.

**It remembers between conversations.** Tell it your points balance, your renewal date, which card you prefer for travel, and it still knows three weeks later.

**Two boundaries, stated on the page:**
It only works on your money. Ask it anything else and it will politely decline. And when it doesn't have the data, it says so rather than filling the gap.

> **Before:** forty minutes across three browser tabs working out whether the fee is disputable.
> **After:** one question, one answer, and the dispute filed from the same screen.

## Mission

**H2:**
### What we're actually trying to fix

There's a kind of financial waste that has nothing to do with discipline. It happens to organised people with good jobs and well-managed money. It happens because recovering $45 costs more than $45 to anyone whose time has value, so the correct individual decision, repeated a few hundred times a year, produces a wrong outcome.

Nobody built a product for this because it isn't a technology problem in the usual sense. It's an execution problem. Someone has to actually sit through the retention flow, actually file the dispute, actually remember the credit expires on the 31st. Automation finally got good enough to do that reliably, and that's the entire reason Amalgamic exists.

Our test for anything we build: does this reduce the number of things you have to do, or does it just show you more things you should be doing? If it's the second, we don't ship it.

# SUPPORTED BANKS

**H1:** Where Amalgamic connects

We don't integrate with banks one at a time. Two regulated data providers do that work, which is why coverage reaches more than 12,000 US institutions rather than a handful of national names.

**Plaid** handles transaction and balance data across national banks, regionals, credit unions and brokerages. Larger institutions use OAuth, so you sign in on your bank's own page.

**Spinwheel** handles credit profile and liability data: cards, loans, lines of credit and balances that transaction feeds tend to miss.

- Nothing found: We went through twelve months and found nothing worth chasing. That's a good result, and it's the honest answer. We'll keep watching from here.
- No subscriptions: No recurring charges on this card. If you know of one we've missed, tell us and we'll look again.
- No insights: Nothing changed that's worth your attention. We'll say something when it does.

*The "nothing found" state matters more than it looks. The hero promised to show what you're owed. If the answer is zero, this line is the one that decides whether the person stays.*

**Bill pay**
- Confirm: Paying [$X] to [card] from [account]. This goes to your issuer, nowhere else.
- Reconnect: [Bank] needs you to sign in again before we can pay from it. About a minute, and your due date is [date].
- Minimum warning: Paying the minimum leaves [$X] carrying interest. Ask the assistant what that costs over a year.

**Errors**
- Connection expired: Your bank needs you to sign in again. Banks ask periodically. About a minute.
- Sync failed: We couldn't reach [bank] just now. We'll keep trying and let you know if it doesn't resolve.

**Assistant**
- Placeholder: Ask about a charge, a statement, or a card.
- Guardrail: I only work on your money. For anything else you'll want a different assistant.
- No data: I don't have that in your connected accounts. Connecting [account type] would let me answer it.

# CLAIMS TO VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING

Each of these appears in this copy or on your current site, and each needs someone to confirm it. In fintech, an unverifiable marketing claim is a regulatory problem, not just a credibility one.

**Numbers currently live that I haven't carried over.** "Avg. Monthly Recovery $240+", "Tasks Handled/Mo 14.2", "System Uptime 99.9%", "Avg setup 1m 14s". If these come from real user data, publish them with an asterisk explaining the sample. If they're projections, take them down. A number as precise as 14.2 invites exactly the scrutiny it can't survive.

**Every row in the proof table.** This is the most checkable content on the site and the most damaging if it isn't true.

**"12,000+ US institutions."** Confirm against current Plaid and Spinwheel coverage.

**"We never train AI models on your data."** Confirm against your actual Vertex AI configuration and Google Cloud data processing terms, not just your intent.

**Bill payment.** Now that it's a headline feature, disclosure obligations change. Confirm with counsel who the processor is, whether a money transmission question arises, and what the footer needs to say.

**Flat pricing, no percentage of recoveries.** Load-bearing on the differentiator page and in the differentiation section. Confirm it's the model you're committing to.

**Disputes filed on the user's behalf.** Worth a conversation with counsel about how third-party dispute submission is characterised, what authorisation you capture, and how you describe it publicly. FCBA dispute rights sit with the cardholder. The "#AMX-992" example needs an illustrative label wherever it appears.

**Assistant behaviour claims.** "Cites where the answer came from" and "says so when it doesn't have the data" are promises your evals have to hold. A visitor who watches the assistant confabulate once will discount everything else on the page.

**"Nothing else does this" claims in section 7.** Check them against Rocket Money, Monarch, Copilot and CardPointers as they stand today. Category-exclusivity claims age badly and are the easiest thing for a competitor to have you correct.

**Merchant cancellation coverage.** Any count you publish will be checked. Publish a floor, not a ceiling.
